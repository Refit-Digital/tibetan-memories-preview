The cook

Namdi learnt this food at home, and in a monastery

He was born in Riwoche, in Kham, in 1976. Life was hard, and he and his brothers and sisters all worked from a young age. There are about twelve of them, he thinks, though he can't say exactly. He left the Tibetan plateau at twenty.

He made his way to Belgium by way of Nepal and India, and spent years working split shifts, six days a week, in a Michelin recommended Italian restaurant in Antwerp. He never learnt Flemish or French. He does know a good few insults in Sardinian.

Some Tibetan staples don't travel well. Tsampa, roasted barley, is a hard sell to anyone who didn't grow up on it. So he kept what works and built the rest around fresh green vegetables, which the plateau never had much of and the West has in abundance. Produce often comes from family and friends' allotments.

A craft and a meditation.

That's how he describes making torma, the coloured offerings built from edible ingredients that he learnt in his monastery. The same attention goes into a tray of momos. He sometimes brings torma to the stall to show people. They're never for sale.

The food is vegan because of what he was taught there, not because of a trend. In his words, the family would rather not profit from others' suffering.

Privacy

This explains what happens to information about you when you visit this site or get in touch. It is written to be read, not to cover us.

Who is responsible

Cookies, only if you say yes

When you first visit, a panel at the foot of the screen asks whether we may use Google Analytics. Until you press Accept, nothing from Google Analytics loads and no cookies are set.

If you accept, Google Analytics sets two cookies, _ga and _ga_09XLR18Z2H, which last up to two years. They let it tell a returning visitor from a new one.

Your answer, yes or no, is kept in your browser's storage for six months so we do not ask on every visit; after that we ask again. Keeping it is what lets the site respect your choice, so it needs no consent of its own. Nothing else is stored on your device: no advertising tags, no tracking pixels.

You can change your mind at any time with Cookie settings at the foot of the page. If you withdraw, Google Analytics stops and its two cookies are deleted.

What is collected, and why

  • Visits to this page, if you accept. Google Analytics counts visits and which buttons people press, such as the phone number, the email address or sending the enquiry form. We see totals, not people, and Google states that Google Analytics does not store IP addresses. The reports sit in an account run by franco.solutions, who built this site and look after it for us. The lawful basis is your consent, which you can withdraw at any time.
  • The calendar of dates. The dates on this page come from our public Google Calendar. Loading it means Google receives your IP address and basic details about your browser.
  • The typeface. The site's typeface is served from this site itself, so loading it tells nobody else you visited.
  • Hosting. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, and like any web host GitHub's servers see the IP address of everyone who asks for a page.
  • Enquiries, by form, email or phone. If you send the enquiry form, write or ring about a booking we keep what you send: your name, how to reach you, the date, the place, and whatever you tell us about your event. We use it to answer you and to run the booking. The lawful basis is taking steps at your request before entering into a contract, and afterwards our legitimate interest in keeping a record of work we have done. The form is delivered to our email by Web3Forms, a form service, which also keeps a copy of each message on its own servers.

What we do not do

The enquiry form only sends us your message. There is no account to create, no payment taken on this site, no newsletter, no advertising and no profiling. We do not build a picture of you, we do not make automated decisions about you, and we do not sell or share anyone's details for anyone else's purposes. We do not send marketing emails or texts; if that ever changed we would ask you first, as the Privacy and Electronic Communications (EC Directive) Regulations 2003 require.

Who else sees it

Google, for the calendar, our email (a Gmail account) and, only if you accept, the analytics; Web3Forms, which delivers the enquiry form to that email; franco.solutions, who look after the site and read the analytics reports; and GitHub, for the hosting. Nobody else, unless we are required by law to hand something over.

Where it goes

Google and GitHub are United States companies, and Web3Forms keeps its copies of form messages with Amazon Web Services, so your information may be handled outside the United Kingdom. Google and GitHub state that they use the safeguards UK data protection law requires for those transfers, and Web3Forms describes its service as GDPR compliant.

How long it is kept

Analytics reports are counts, not records about individuals. Google Analytics deletes the underlying visit data after the period set in the account, at most 14 months. Enquiry emails, including those sent with the form, are kept in our Gmail account for three years after the event, then deleted. Web3Forms deletes its own copy of a form message after at most three years.
